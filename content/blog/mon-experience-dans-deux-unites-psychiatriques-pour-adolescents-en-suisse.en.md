Hello everyone, I'm currently working on a book about my experience in teen psychiatric units, specifically from the French side of Switzerland.

In this blog post, I will breefly talk about the effects after my stays, most of it happened 5 to 7 years ago, but it still negatively affects me to this day. Think of this blog post as a very short version of my experience if I can't release the book for some reason.

The book will talk in detail about my experience, so much so that I might get into trouble, even by censoring names. There are very few units, so finding which one I'm talking about is easy. Of course, do not harass or contact anyone, even if you think they deserve it. It won't do anything good, and believe me, I know that very well from the mistakes I made while trying to get justice.

## My experience

I went three times to a unit I'll call "M". My first stay was in may-june 2019, the second in november-december 2019, and the third in april-may 2020. The first and second lasted 2–3 months and the third 4–5 months. Since I started skipping college because of something I felt there, my mother or a psycologist/psychiatrist sent me there.

Unit M is very small, it has two floors. The first is the unit itself and the second has a classroom, rooms dedicated to therapy and a waiting room. The unit has seven rooms for patients, one for urgencies, two doubles and four singles.

When I entered the unit, I felt a sense of déjà vu. Turns out, I already visited the unit a couple of years before, but didn't get admitted. A lot of weird things happened when I was there. It was one of those places that seemed "cool" from the outside, with a brochure advertising a school, therapies and group activites. To me, it felt like those activites were a fascade to entice or reassure parents, or a marketing strategy if you think of it like a shop. "Unit M isn't just a unit, it's way more than that... We've got X, Y and Z !", you get the point.

The weirdest one was the classroom, the teacher loved natural medecine, and maybe Indian culture as well. Every morning, we would watch a 5-minute YouTube video to help us meditate. A ball went up and down with breathing sound effects, the Om symbol on the left filled up after each cycle. She also had a Koshi chime she would move around the classroom while we closed our eyes. The goofiest exercice was brain gym. A patient is at the "piano", which was actually an old Windows 7 computer with YouTube. While the music played, we did mouvements that are supposed to "train our brains".

As for the actual work in the classroom, it depends. Most of the time, your school would communicate with the teacher, so you can get your textbooks or similar work. If that wasn't possible, the teacher would just give you whatever was there. When that happened to me during my third stay, it was too easy for me and I felt like it was a waste of time.

Imagine telling your parents what really happens in the classroom, they might believe some parts, but not everything. When we think of a classroom, most of us will think of our own experiences at school. When I got the brochure, I thought it would be the same thing in terms of exercices & tests. I knew it would be a bit different, but not that much. "Sure, it's goofy, but it's not that deep", I thought. Of course, those activites are not advertised in the brochure. It's like the teacher wanted to do things she couldn't before, and the staff didn't give a shit because it didn't interfere with their work. I knew other units had a school as well, but some didn't have art-therapy. When you go to the unit and see it for yourself, you know what it means, but to an outsider, it's surprising and "informative".

Before this, I was that introverted kid at school. My parents divorced when I was 11-12. I was diagnosed with ASD way back and my mother was obsessed with it. She wanted to do everything right to an extreme level. She kept telling me how autism isn't that bad. I didn't think like her, I thought she made a bigger deal then it should be and that maybe my diagnosis was false or exagerated. When I told her what I really thought, she told me stories about succesful people with autism and how she does all of this for me. I stopped trying after a while, nodding my head so that she would stop talking about it.

I still remember the day when my life changed, it was in college, I was getting my stuff from my locker for the next class. It was packed, someone I knew was talking with two guys. I go behind him, feel something touch my shoulder hard, I'm surprised and look behind, that's when I understood. That thing that touched me, it was his body, those two guys were beating him up. It reminded me of a fear I had at home, being beaten or killed by my little brother. Most of the time, it was just screams, but from time to time, he would get violent, like by beating my mother. Eventually, he got into an institution, but since he went home on weekends, his rage just ended up getting more & more extreme, the only thing I could do was shut up and let it slide.

I couldn't fight back, I knew I was weak. Those awful feelings, my body giving up, having trouble breathing, my ears ringing, I knew them all too well when I tried. When his behaviour became more extreme, I wanted to kill him so that the screams would stop. To me, he was a threat, and he always got away with it. When I would complain to my parents, they told me that "it's in the past", that "he can't control himself" and that I should not talk to anyone about what goes on in the house, because it's "private".

To give you an idea of my worries, one day while on vacation, I was watching anime on my phone on the 3rd floor, my grandparents and guests were eating downstairs. Since I didn't have a stand for my phone, I placed it on the electric roller shutter near a big window and sat on a chair to watch. The shutter is controlled with up & down buttons, they are placed in the entrance of the room. Out of nowhere, my brother goes to the 3rd floor, opens the door to the room and presses the up & down buttons to get my attention. At first, I thought he just wanted to annoy me, so I got up, went near the entrance, and saw that this wasn't the case.

He stood there with a knife and the smile of a psychopath, he tells me "I'm going to kill you..." with a calm tone. I get out of the room by luring him into a spot, then running to another, I tried to go to the 2nd floor, but he locked the door, so I locked myself in another room and yelled for about an hour. My grandparents tried to convince him to open the door, but it didn't work, a guest did manage to convince him by acting like the police. After that, I tried to distance myself by refusing to be near him, but like always, my parents told me I should let it go because "it's in the past" and because he "changed".

I would say my vision of life got me into this, back then, I viewed the world as a beautiful place with ups & downs. After everything I went through, my vision changed negatively. It's not the fault of the dude that got beat up, he doesn't even know what happened to me. I can't really say anyone is to blame, and even if there was, I don't think anything good would happen if it was known. It would just be another day filled with hate, what ifs and regrets.

The first stay felt strange, I didn't know what was normal, illegal or immoral anymore. I thought, "Maybe I'm the one that's crazy, so I need to shut my mouth". Let's just say it was a shock for me. I saw a girl getting tackled to the ground for nothing, adults making mocking gestures while a boy was crying behind a locked room, and an unstable worker that made everyone, including the adults, uncomfortable. Since I could go home on the weekends, I talked about it to my best friend, she told me "Be careful, this looks like a cult...". I didn't take her seriously, but a few years later, some things I've seen and heard definitly give off a cult vibe, more for the workers then the patients, like a family if you see what I mean. The second stay felt slightely more serious because I started skipping my new school. They looked annoyed, but not too much. I thought this would help me to never get admitted to Unit M in the future, but I was wrong...

The third stay was the worst of them all, especially because of an incident. It was something I saw online that shocked me, I told it to my mother to free my mind before going to bed. When I got admitted this time, she suddenly revealed this out of nowhere because she was "worried". I didn't expect this at all, with no trust between them and me, the first meetings were heated. No mather what I said, they were right and I was wrong. A staff member told me that people like my kind don't belong in society, as well as other things. I understood why he told me these things, but I was not okay with him acting this way without any substantial proof, especially with a subject this serious. For a while, they didn't talk about the incident, but I was terrified, I couldn't believe that doing what I thought was right would get twisted in a way where I was a criminal. If people heard that version of the incident, at least one person would've tried to beat me up. After all, this is one of the worst crimes someone can commit in this world.

I couldn't finish my scholarship, which largely restricted my job oportunities. Since the unit "helped me" adapt to my new school by making phone calls, aranging a few dates where I could attend school while I was still there, and the small dormitory when I started skipping school, having that opportunity ruined made them angry. In their eyes, I fucked up their "hard work" and my future. They would yell at me things like "This is your fault! You're the one who fucked it all up!". I was thinking "Yeah alright, can you stop yelling at me like you're my mother? I didn't ask you for anything, you're scaring me...". I could sometimes see their hands slightly shaking from anger, it was terrifying.

I had my reasons as to why I skipped my new school, to make it short, my mother was, in a way, happy that college didn't go well for me, because she could send me to a specialized school. She never told me this outright, she did tell me her opinion which I didn't agree with at all, but I kept quiet to avoid trouble. "Why is it the students job to adapt to the establishment and not the other way around? I think it's their job to adapt to them!". I did go to a specialized school for a few months around 2009-2010 before going back to a regular one, the adults in charge told my mother that going the normal route would be the best choice for my future. In normal school, I had a couple of teachers helping me, in college, I also had a specialized class, but that was it. Aside from a couple of kids mocking me from time to time, it wasn't a nightmare. When I started skiping college, I think it was the perfect opportunity for her to show these people that "I needed this", even if that's never what I wanted. I wanted to go back to college, but that was impossible now.

When I told my mother I wanted to go back to college, she was angry at me, "I sent you to this school and you want to go back to college? (laugh)". I didn't know if it would've been possible eitherway, but it definitly made me feel like shit. I didn't feel like I belonged there, to me, most of the people I saw genuinly needed it, like their life would be a nightmare or litteraly impossible for them in a regular school. And there I was because I felt something in college and got scared. The staff at the school really wanted to help me, but I couldn't tell them the truth. I had no guarantee that they wouldn't inform my mother, something I saw her demanding on the phone to my brother's institution once. Her request was rejected because she didn't need to know everything, but it was clear to me she saw it as a right which couldn't be rejected, even if the reason was invalid for them.

When I visited the small dormotory, I wanted to cry so bad, but didn't want to because these people didn't deserve it. When one of them was showing us the dorms, I didn't know what to do anymore and fell on the ground, trying to cry, the tears weren't falling, it felt fake. The only times I've cried were when people really pushed me to my limit, like those cases with my brother or at unit M. They gave me water and tried to appease me. Once I got in my mother's car to go back home, I felt like I was going to die or lose everything. My mother was angry, she told me this would be the last time I would make a mess like this. At that point, it was over for me, if I couldn't go back to college like before, it wasn't worth it.

Since the school was in another state, I would finish school at 18 or a few more years instead of at 15-16. I couldn't even imagine myself being there for that long. I tried to make things easier by telling them things I didn't like at school, and they couldn't do anything. The only exemple I can think of is the gym, there were way too many hyperactive kids and I didn't feel good. Of course, that's not their fault, so I talked about it to the teacher. She said she couldn't exempt me from gym because it's mandatory.

The last time I went to school, teachers were trying to understand what they could do to help me. I was in a room with the teacher of my class and my mother, he asked me "What's wrong in my class?", I wanted to tell him "Nothing", but couldn't, so my brain tried to generate an excuse like an AI chatbot, it was so pathetic it didn't make any sense and just made me look like an idiot.

Near the end, teachers met me in a room. I cried, I wanted to tell them the truth, but couldn't because my mother was there, or would know later anyway. After that meeting, I couldn't go back to school. I don't doubt the teachers would've said anything to my mother if I told them not to, but even the slightest chance could be catastrophic for me. If I told them what I really thought like "My mother is obsessed with autism", "I'm scared of being killed by my little brother", "I don't feel like I belong here" and "I want to go back to college", I could get my electronic devices confiscated and have to listen to her propaganda, "I'm doing all of this for you!". I couldn't take the risk, otherwise I would've gone crazy!

## The aftermath

Everytime I got out of the unit, I felt more & more suicidal. I went to my father's house when I got out the 3rd time, but even there, the feeling of having no control over my future still followed me. I had a guardian assigned to me by protective services. In Switzerland, there are multiple types of them, I had the least severe one. This woman would call me from time to time, telling me about places where she could send me for an internship. One day, I felt really suicidal, since I was supposed to go to a new internship, I told her this wasn't the right time. She told me I should stop being so negative and sent me there anyway. Like at unit M, it felt like talking to a wall instead of a guardian, like I had no control over my life anymore. I was able to get out of this place by talking with the ones managing the dormitory, when one of them tried to contact her, someone told her she got into a car accident. A few days later, I was invited to a meeting with people working on my case. A woman was asking me all sorts of questions like "Why did you quit?", I felt like I made a mistake, it was weird. Before I turned 18, three guardians had replaced her.

At that time, I started to feel like my ASD diagnosis was in fact real. My mother just didn't know how to react and got lost to the point where she hurt me. She felt like the whole world was against her, protective services, my dad, my brother's institution, you get the point. Small things shattered my perception, "Oh well of course I'm sensitive to noises, if I come up to someone and scream in his ear, he's going to be sensitive as well, doesn't mean he's autistic!", "I want to work a regular computer job, but I never even got the chance to apply for an internship at a regular company! I wanted to, but the people in charge of helping me kept refusing and told me this would not work out without even trying!", "Sure, maybe I legally can't, but why are they blindly trusting other people's word over mine?".

One day while I was on vacation, there was some sort of event where loud sounds similar to fireworks were constant. When the noise started, I put my hands over my ears, it was so bad my dad had to escort me elsewhere, I saw all these people not even being bothered by the noise. For jobs, it was different, I can't really talk about it, but in a nutshell, I got an internship for about 7 months in a specialized place and quit after I got into a conflict with my brother over the phone while I was in the dormotory. The woman in charge of my case got angry with me on the phone, because that was the longest internship I had gotten in my life. I could tell she felt bad for me, but like the rest, I don't think she would've accepted a no for an answer, even if it came from me.

It was the same thing I felt with my mother and all these people, they couldn't accept that at some point, certain things aren't possible anymore. I wanted to work so badly, but couldn't because my case was stuck and getting work elsewhere could get me in trouble. I was even ready to try food delivery, not sure if I could do it physically, but I was open to give it a go. I realized something that I couldn't accept for a while, I couldn't even work normally on anything at home. I felt sick mentally, whether it was on my computer or outside, my life had no meaning anymore.

Unit M literally made me a worse person then before, I was able to play more video games and get out more a couple of years after, but I wasn't the same anymore. Anytime I did anything, I heard voices without actually hearing them, "You're such a fucking loser, living with your dad, drinking coke and eating junk food, just kill yourself already!", "Your mother should've locked that fucking computer in a cage! Why are you still doing this? Such a waste!", "People like you don't belong in society, you stay at home getting bennefits while I work my ass off and my taxes pay for little shits like you! If I could, I would kill every single one of you! Just leave this country if you don't like it!".

Nobody actually told me these things word for word, maybe similar in some way online or in real life. It was a mix of things I heard about me and my feelings. I had no future, but my dad and the people around me didn't believe it. No mather what I said, they couldn't accept my reality. Even going outside made me feel worse, whether something happened or not, it reminded me of the life I lost. I still force myself to go out because I want to believe things will change, but for now, it still hasn't. Every day feels like my body is losing energy. On the outside, I'm alive, but on the inside, I'm already dead, like I'm being forced to live.

For a long time, I lied to everyone about my true feelings. This is mainly why I'm making this blog post and my book, it's about the most negative and dangerous effect I've seen in psychiatry, I call it "The obligation to lie". When I went to unit M for the first time and got out, I thought to myself, "If they send you here again, don't let them get you alive, run away and jump of a bridge, it's better to die then relive this shit...". After leaving the dormitory, I went to unit P in the south. Since I went volontarly, I could get out with my dad's permission. In the end, I left after 5 days.

The dude in my room was someone that knew me a few years ago, I didn't remember him instantly, he had to remind me. One day, they put him in the locked room and gave him strong medication, he was like a zombie when the effects kicked in. I've seen this before at unit M, but seing it happen to someone you know or remember, it's not the same. I didn't know why it affected me so much. When I first got into my room, he asked me if I remembered him and I literally couldn't, maybe it's because he got into the same classes as me in college when I wasn't there anymore. The workers were nicer, even though I can't say much for 5 days. The first week was less shocking than my first at unit M. I felt more understood, but also felt like some workers were giving an act and would slowly drop it as time went on.

When I became an adult, my dad was okay with me having a guardian while I wasn't. I didn't manage to express myself properly when the protective services wanted to talk to me. What they didn't tell my dad, was that it wouldn't be the type of guardian he thought of. The type they chose was the one just below the highest level, the one that manages wealth. It was a mess. I went three times to adult units, two at unit B, which became M since they moved out a few weeks before I arrived, and the adult side of P, which was bigger since they were split across multiple units across the hospital. It was way worse than what I imagined, I experienced a few fucked up things, as well as other stuff I could've if I had gotten admitted earlier. I heard a worker got fired when I got admitted, he did really disgusting things, he worked there for at least more than a year from what I've heard. I don't want to talk in detail about what I could have experienced there if I had gotten admitted earlier, all I can say is that it would be related to sex and physical violence. Thankfully, I was able to get out of there both times after 5–7 days.

The last one I went to was unit P. I remember freaking out in the emergency room of the hospital in my city when my dad saw suicidal behaviour from me. Someone injected me something powerful, I woke up being moved on a stretcher to another room, then got moved to another unit inside P because there weren't any rooms left. They asked me questions, but I didn't know what to say. I thought I lost part of my memories, which scared the shit out of me. Even worse, I got admitted under PAFA, which is involontary hospitalisation, so there was a high chance I could stay there for months. The first night was and still is the worst one of my life, since I was asleep between the injection and when I woke up at P, I had a hard time sleeeping, if only it was just that... For some reason, my body couldn't stop slightly shaking on its own when I was laying down on the bed, I tried to ask for medications to help me, but it didn't help much.

I was able to get out of the unit after a week by acting in a good way. I was even ready to pay train tickets to get home if my father couldn't come. I don't remember if the train pass from my internship was still valid, but I packed my bags, took a bus towards the station and looked at the bus window thinking, "This time will truely be the last I ever come back to these places...". With everything I've gone through, working on anything seemed like it could hurt me or kill me. I forbid myself from even thinking about certain things like trying to make money, no mather if it may have been possible or not. If it could get this bad, I couldn't do it, period.

Because of what happened during my third stay at Unit M, I cut off all contact with my mother, and unfortunatly had to do the same with my grandparents from her side and my sister. One of the staff members told my mother something he assured me he wouldn't, because if he did, he could get into legal trouble. There was something strange about my mother's behaviour, she didn't seem to care that I didn't want to go back home with her, which never happened before. I was reassured in a way, but still felt weird. I couldn't connect the dots back then, but 1–3 months after I left, I heard her say something that took me months to process.

I was in a sort of small specialized place where teens go instead of stricter facilities. For some reason, they wanted to invite my mother, which I refused or told them it was a bad idea. When an employee saw how uncomfortable I was around her, he told me something like "It's that bad?", I said "Yeah...". They decided to stop, the last thing I needed to do was go upstairs to a meeting with the woman who ran the place and my mother. I was very uncomfortable, at one point, she started crying and said something I didn't expect. After 6–12 months, I finally understood what her words meant. The staff member that assured me he wouldn't say anything to her, lied to me.

I felt it was strange from the start, but couldn't do anything. Why would he tell me this if it wasn't necessary at the time? This is something patients know from the start, "Everything you say here stays here". Of course, there's a limit to that. If someone says something worrying, then it's a different story. Problem is, my case didn't fall into that category. The thing I told him was "When I see her, I want to vomit...". Not because of her looks, but because of everything I've gone through. I was supposed to be present at a meeting with my mother and the two staff members, since I didn't want to go, I told him why it wasn't a good idea. He told me I would not need to be present at the meeting, that he wouldn't and couldn't say what I told him to her. A few days after the meeting, that's when her strange behaviour started.

There was one piece of info I just couldn't believe for a while... During my first stay, me and another patient visited a new place that the bouldering teacher opened (or helped open). He was the friend of a worker who came every thursday to teach us bouldering. Since we were just two patients in a van, the worker and his friend started talking like we weren't there. Eventually, his friend brings up a case of a teen who tried to sue the unit, "What happened with that teen that tried to sue the unit?". His response felt unreal to me, "Oh you know, cases agains't us don't go anywhere". At that time, I never had a direct experience with the justice system. I thought that all those cases where the system didn't work must've been misreported by the press or a huge misunderstanding, you can already guess how shocked I was when I went to the police.

After I understood that a staff member lied to me and some other events, I went to the police station in the same town as the hospital. The first thing the man at the reception desk told me was "Don't do this, it's not worth it...". I pushed through, he said I would need my dad's permission, so I got it. Even after all that, all they could do was leave a note on his file because it wasn't recent. They acted like I should forget the past and concentrate on my life, something my dad did over and over again when I talked to him about what happened in the unit. That just made me more frustrated inside, I couldn't. It's not like I woke up one day and told myself, "I have nothing to do with my life, so I'll make it about the unit!". Before, I thought that if something happened to me, I could rely on the authorities, I was so naive it's not even funny...

Even when I turned 18, other things happened to me that made me lose trust in the autorities. Certain people and places are untouchable, it's unfortunate, but it's true. To this day, I still don't know why he told my mother that I felt sick around her, and not even saying it right. If I had to guess, she was the one that pressured him into revealing things, because in her mind, it's her right as a parent to know. The staff member was very close to retirement, he knew very well that even if he broke the rules, I would be the one in the wrong.

The incident was the last straw between my mother and I. I tried to maintain a good relation so that I could still talk to my grandparents, but after a year, I knew it wasn't gonna work out. Knowing that, I made my mother think I wanted to talk to her again, got my Gaming PC back, then blocked her again, that was the only way I could convince her. One day, I did something stupid during a phone call with her, regretted it but was too late, then got into an argument with her. She firmly denied having mixed up my words when I got admitted the third time, I tried reminding her of the exact words I said that night, but nothing could change her mind. She did that all the time, if she said something, it was the absolute truth. Normally, it was for stupid shit, but this was different. This could ruin my life or end it, for something I never said.

As hard as that was, I knew I couldn't live my life like this, so I blocked her everywhere. As I expected, my grandparents from my mother's side told me to unblock her because she was sad. I told them the truth, and they still didn't believe me. "That's not possible, our girl would never do that!", "You must've mixed stuff up when you were at the hospital". They clearly showed me that if I wasn't on good terms with her, it wasn't worth talking to them. When my dad would visit their appartment with me, they wouldn't talk about my mother, but when he went to the toilet, they talked behind his back. Things like "I feel sad for him...", "Your mother loves you, you know?", "You should be nicer to her, she's your mother you know?", "You should've obayed your mother, you know?". My sister told me I was hurting my mother because I blocked her, as much as I didn't want to, I had to block her as well even if it wasn't her fault. In the end, I cut off all contact with most of my family. I unblocked my sister recently because things changed, but that's it.

## Why now?

You may be thinking, "Why talk about it now? This happened years ago so unit M, P, and or the system as a whole might have changed". My main reason is the following, I don't want this to be forgotten or deleted. I know this looks like someone talking way too much about their life on the Internet, but I don't want this to be about me, I just don't know how I could express it without all this seeming "fake" or "out of context", so I'm just doing it the best I can.

My goal is to inform about my experience, which is far tamer than others, but still isn't excusable, to give a voice to those that can't. Whether something changes or not as a result, that's not my job. I don't want to die one day without a trace of what I went through. Even if the system gets "fixed" later, which I highly doubt, if these issues are forgotten in the future, things can roll back or get worse at any time. I really don't think this issue is a top priority for most countries, not even in the top 50. Just look at the list of problems "normal" people experience, this shit means nothing to them and that's normal, because most of the time, we only know what it feels like when it happens to us.

My opinion isn't that X unit is bad, or that all units are bad, if I told you that, it would be a lie. Believe it or not, some people actually had a positive experience in unit M with the same staff as when I was there, some even wanted to stay longer, but got rejected. When patients left, some told workers and patients how much this place helped them, but when they're finally outside, they say the exact opposite or nothing. Some told the truth, but avoided causing trouble near the end so they could go home like planned, some felt mixed or didn't know how they felt yet, and some actually got better.

My issue with all this is straightforward, when someone's experience is so bad that they are ready to end it all, why should the good experiences of others discredit the bad ones? That makes no sense! If I had an amazing experience in a unit and years later, I see that someone killed themselves because of something the workers in the unit said or did, my positive experience would mean nothing. At the end of the day, someone felt so bad they prefered dying over staying, when that place was supposed to make them better, that's what really frustrates me. I saw teenagers all around the world making jokes online of similar situations, things like "this is my new room after talking too much". I'm not gonna lie, that made me laugh, because that was and still is a genuine fear of mine.

Sending a mentally unwell person to a unit is logical from a moral and legal perspective, but when the quality of the units vastely differ and consequences rarely occur to workers and staff, that's where things get dangerous. When I get admitted to any unit, I know that if anything happens to me, I won't be protected most of the time. It was simple, you shut up, you lie, you let them talk, and eventually, you get out.

During the 3rd stay at unit M, I saw a "check-up" for the first time, I was surprised, but knew it wouldn't change anything. To my knowledge, this wasn't because of a report, but because it's mandatory every X years. They knew exactly when they would come, so the workers and staff just held back their problematic beheviors until they left. I never saw them myself, but knew they were there because we were informed by the staff. From what I've heard from patients, they were just standing there observing their meetings, and that's it.
